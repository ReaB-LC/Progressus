## Description

**The pilgrim always reaches the Celestial City — the question is how.**

*Progressus* is a cooperative journey game for 1 to 4 players, based on John Bunyan's classic allegory *The Pilgrim's Progress* (1678).

Players do not control separate heroes. Together they guide a single pilgrim, Christian, from the City of Destruction to the Celestial City. Each player takes one faculty of his soul: **Understanding**, **Will**, **Conscience** or **Affections**. Each faculty has its own virtues, damage track and passive ability. Only the Will can move the pilgrim; the Understanding reads what lies ahead; the Conscience remembers the Promises and answers the Accuser; the Affections lend strength to the others.

At the heart of the game is a **Grace engine**. Every morning brings new Grace, unearned. Beyond that, Grace comes through the means God has appointed: the Word, Prayer and Fellowship, and, when the whole soul gathers, the **Prayer Meeting**. But the means are not machines: Grace arrives through the **Wind deck**, sometimes in abundance, sometimes not at all, sometimes as healing instead (John 3:8). At every crossroads the pilgrim's level of Grace decides the path, never the players. Neglect the means of grace, and the road may lead to Mount Sinai, By-Path Meadow, Doubting Castle or the Flatterer's net.

On the Way, many cards are resolved blind: each faculty secretly commits its virtues, and the group may say *whether* it will help, but never *how much*.

The 58-space board follows the road through 14 key locations, including the Slough of Despond, the Cross, the Hill Difficulty, the Palace Beautiful, the Valley of the Shadow of Death, Vanity Fair, the Delectable Mountains and the River of Death. A fixed **Chronicle deck** tells the story in order, while five **Stage decks**, the Rooms and the Mountains bring varied events, including episodes from Part II. Along the way the pilgrim may receive the Roll, the Garments and the Mark at the Cross, put on the Armour of God (Ephesians 6), and walk with companions such as Faithful and Hopeful.

Every wrong turn, every night in Doubting Castle and every faculty brought low costs precious time. The players' goal is to arrive within 40 rounds. Arrive early and earn an *Abundant Entrance*; arrive in time and hear *"Well done, good and faithful servant"*; arrive late and the game is lost, for the pilgrim is *"saved, yet so as by fire"*.

The rulebook closes with **"In the Light of the Word"**, a chapter that explains each mechanic from Scripture and the historic Reformed and Puritan faith.
